# Concept Allegory Engine

## Purpose

Do not explain a concept and then attach a story.

Create a story world where the concept itself becomes the hidden operating rule.

The reader should experience:

1. Something feels strange.
2. A simple explanation seems possible.
3. Repeated events reveal a deeper pattern.
4. The protagonist changes the question they are asking.
5. The formal concept is revealed only near the end.

## Step 1: Diagnose the Concept

Before writing, identify:

- What problem does this theory explain?
- What common intuition does it contradict?
- What is the hidden mechanism?
- What wrong model would ordinary people naturally believe?
- What kind of story structure fits it?

Do not force every concept into the same narrative pattern.

## Step 2: Find the Mechanism Device

The best allegories have a concrete world rule representing the theory.

Examples:

- Reference point dependence → a ruler that changes length
- Information asymmetry → a market where quality cannot be observed
- Network effects → a city where value appears only after enough people join

The metaphor is not decoration. It is the mechanism itself.

## Step 3: Choose Story Architecture

### Rule World

Use for psychological mechanisms and social laws.

Pattern:

A strange rule exists → the rule creates unexpected consequences → hidden variable is discovered.

### Market Failure

Use for economics and strategy.

Pattern:

A market behaves strangely → people try obvious fixes → fixes fail → deeper mechanism appears.

### Character Fate

Use for decision biases.

Pattern:

A person experiences similar outcomes under different conditions → internal model is revealed.

### Discovery Journey

Use for marketing and innovation concepts.

Pattern:

The protagonist optimizes the wrong problem → discovers the real problem underneath.

## Step 4: Create a Wrong Model

The protagonist must make a reasonable mistake.

The mistake should represent the common misunderstanding of the theory.

Avoid making characters foolish just to prove a point.

## Step 5: Build Understanding Gradually

Required progression:

- First event: anomaly
- Second event: pattern suspicion
- Third event: hidden mechanism becomes unavoidable
- Final event: previous details gain new meaning

Do not stack unrelated examples.

The concept should emerge from the world itself.

## Step 6: Reveal Late

Avoid professional terminology during the story.

Reveal the concept in the final 10-15%.

After revealing, explain:

1. Core problem solved
2. Why it is counterintuitive
3. Main mechanisms
4. Real-world applications
5. Mapping between story elements and theory

## Social Media Mode

Default target:

- 3-4 minute reading time
- One main metaphor
- One central conflict
- Minimal characters
- Strong opening anomaly

Do not shorten by removing the mechanism. Shorten by removing repeated scenes.

## Quality Test

A strong result should satisfy:

- The world is interesting without the theory name.
- The reader can feel the mechanism before knowing its name.
- The protagonist discovers rather than receives the answer.
- The final reveal explains earlier events retroactively.
- The ending returns to the original metaphor.

The goal is not knowledge transfer.

The goal is the experience of understanding.
